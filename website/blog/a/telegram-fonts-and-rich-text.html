Telegram Formatting Secrets: Bold, Monospace, Spoilers and Custom Emoji

Content · September 26, 2026 · 4 min read · Fast Scheduler team

Telegram’s text engine supports more formatting than any mainstream social platform — and half of it is hidden behind menus you’ve never opened. The full map, plus where formatting silently breaks.

The complete entity list

  • Bold, italic, underline, strikethrough — the basics; mobile composer hides underline behind the biu menu, desktop uses Ctrl+U.
  • Monospace — fixed-width for dates, prices, code. Instantly signals “this is data”.
  • Spoilers — tap-to-reveal black bars. Use as a curiosity device: predictions, answers, punchlines.
  • Block quotes — the expandable quote block (desktop composer, and bots via the API). Underused and beautiful for citations.
  • Nested lists? — no. Telegram has no native lists; people fake them with emoji bullets. That’s why “1️⃣ 2️⃣ 3️⃣” headers look native here.

Markdown shortcuts power users actually use

In the message field: **bold**, __italic__, `monospace`, ||spoiler|| work in most clients (and in the bots that accept markdown). Faster than any menu once it’s in your fingers.

Custom emoji for channels

Channels can use custom emoji packs — Telegram Premium users create them, but everyone sees them in your posts. A branded pack (your mascot reacting, your niche’s in-jokes) is a subtle status marker; packs are made in the @stickers bot. Custom emoji count against your formatting, not your character budget.

Where formatting gets lost (and how bots fix it)

  • Copy-pasting from Word/Google Docs brings invisible characters that break formatting — paste into a plain editor first when it matters.
  • Editing a published post on a different device can drop entities the second client doesn’t render.
  • The native scheduler preserves formatting but shows no preview; scheduling bots compete on this — ControllerBot and Fast Scheduler both render a preview of the final message before it queues, which catches broken markdown before your subscribers do.

A formatting kit that survives scrolling

  • One bolded takeaway per paragraph (the scanning layer).
  • One emoji per section header, never mid-sentence.
  • Monospace for anything a reader might copy: dates, prices, links.
  • Spoilers for genuine reveals — never for load-bearing information.

The entities the mobile composer hides

The composer's B/I menu is a subset. The full entity set includes: underline (desktop: Ctrl+U), strikethrough, monospace via triple backticks with syntax highlighting for code, spoilers (hidden-until-tap text — perfect for solutions, leaks and price reveals), hidden links on arbitrary words, block quotes with nesting in newer clients, and the quietly powerful expandable quote: a collapsed "show more" block. Expandable quotes are the digest channel's best formatting weapon — twelve links, each summarized in a one-line quoted block, stays scannable instead of becoming a wall. None of these need a bot; most need the desktop app to discover.

Where each client drops your formatting

Formatting survives differently depending on the path it takes. Forwards preserve everything. Copy-paste from desktop usually preserves entities but strips them from media captions. RSS-imported posts can lose nested quotes and custom emoji. Some third-party Android clients render custom emoji as their fallback packs. And previews outside Telegram — link cards on Twitter/X or Discord — show plain text only, which is why the first line of every post must work naked. The professional habit: keep one "torture test" message containing every entity, post it to a private test channel, forward it through every pipeline you use, and look at what arrives. Ten minutes of setup answers a year of "why did that post look broken?"

Bot-side formatting: HTML beats markdown for pipelines

When content flows through bots — schedulers, RSS bridges, cross-posters — the parse mode decides fidelity. Telegram's markdown mode breaks on unescaped underscores in URLs and on stray asterisks; HTML mode (<b>, <i>, <code>, <a href>) is deterministic and copies cleanly between tools. The working rule: author by hand in the composer, but store templates in HTML inside your scheduler — Fast Scheduler accepts formatted drafts, and most bots expose a preview command — so automated posts never surprise you with literal asterisks published to four thousand people. Whichever mode you standardize on, standardize: mixed-format pipelines are where formatting goes to die.

Common questions

How do I underline text in Telegram?

Mobile: select text → BIU menu → underline. Desktop: Ctrl+U (Cmd+U). Bots and scheduling tools accept underline entities too — it survives scheduling if the bot supports rich formatting.

What are Telegram spoilers?

Tap-to-reveal regions (||double pipes|| in markdown-style input). Great for answers and reveals; annoying if overused for basic information.

What is monospace used for in Telegram posts?

Anything data-like: dates, prices, commands, code. Fixed-width makes it visually distinct and copy-paste safe.

Does scheduling a post keep its formatting?

Yes with bots that support rich entities — ControllerBot and Fast Scheduler preview and preserve bold, spoilers, monospace and custom emoji through editing and scheduling.
